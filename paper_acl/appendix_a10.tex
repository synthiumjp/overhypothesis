\section{Experiment 2, the embedding analysis and the pretrained test (Amendment 10)}\label{app:N}
\setcounter{table}{0}\renewcommand{\thetable}{\thesection\arabic{table}}
\textbf{Registration.} Amendment 10 has three parts: Experiment 2 (Part A), the pretrained test (Part B) and the embedding analysis of the Experiment 1 models (Part C). It was registered after the Amendment 9 results, after its corpora were built and checksummed, and before any of them was used, any of its models was trained, or any pretrained model was run on its items. It answered two confounds raised by a blind review of the Amendment 9 results (by an AI assistant given only the write-up of Experiment 1): new nouns never recurred within a window, and no-shape nouns never appeared in a frame with a shape slot after \emph{is a}. The noun lists for the pretrained test were fixed, with checksums, before the pilot. A pilot on Pythia-410M (never in a test) gave a mean increase of 0.13 nats from the original to the random (untrained norm) embedding, so the smallest effect of interest for H48 was 0.30 nats by the registered rule. One deviation: some Pythia tokenizers define no padding token, so the scoring script falls back to token id 0; padded positions are masked and only the last real token is read, and batched and single-item scores agreed exactly. No score had been produced by the affected models.

\textbf{Experiment 2 design.} Everything not stated is as in Amendment 9 (Appendix~\ref{app:M}). Histories: \emph{fixed}, frame 1 (\emph{A \textit{noun} is a \textit{shape} \textit{colour} \textit{texture} thing}) with the noun's own shape; \emph{absent}, frame 7 (\emph{A \textit{noun} is a \textit{colour} \textit{texture} thing}), which names no shape; \emph{changing-shape}, frame 1 with a shape drawn from the corpus's shape distribution, a new one for every sentence when presented spaced and one per run, redrawn for each run, when presented in bursts. Presentation: \emph{spaced}, one sentence per unit; \emph{bursts}, four consecutive sentences about the noun in one window. Exposures (100 or 1,000) count sentences, so runs and singles are matched in sentences, not in units or spacing. Trained kinds use frame 7 in one sentence in fifteen, so the frame is not new. Three nouns per cell (12 cells) and four unseen nouns; stable-kind (confirmatory) and shifting-kind (exploratory) base corpora; three sizes and ten seeds (60 models). A checker re-read every corpus and verified exposures, units, frames, shapes within runs and window placement; all 40 corpora matched checksums recorded before training.

\textbf{Reanalysis of the Experiment 2 models (exploratory, added after the results).} For every familiar noun in the 60 final models we recorded its input embedding's cosine with its initial embedding (rebuilt from the seed; the rebuild reproduced the unseen nouns' embeddings, which receive no gradient, in every model), its distance from the trained kinds' mean embedding and its norm; the copy score with its own embedding, with a typical embedding and with the embedding of a noun of another history (embeddings swapped between changing- and fixed-shape bursts and between changing-shape bursts and spaced nouns); the copy score with one sentence about a trained kind between context and query (gap), with the context in the frame \emph{Look at the \textit{noun}. It is \textit{shape} and \textit{colour}.} (other frame), and for the context's colour after \emph{A \textit{noun} is a \textit{shape}} (colour); and first-layer attention from the query position to the context's shape, averaged over heads. Full results, including shifting-kind corpora and 100 exposures, are in the supplementary material.

\textbf{The pretrained test.} 44 nouns in 22 pairs, matched on Pile frequency and count/mass type, were coded for colour typicality by two independent AI passes with a fixed prompt before any model was run (exact agreement 94\%, weighted $\kappa$ = 0.96); nouns that are also colour terms or that both passes flagged as polysemous were excluded. Colour-typical nouns are mostly natural kinds, so kind was a covariate. Each noun was read in three neutral frames with each of the eleven basic colour terms that are one token; copy score is the mean over frames and ordered colour pairs of $\log p(c_1 \mid c_1) - \log p(c_1 \mid c_2)$, with colour probabilities renormalised over the colour set. Only the noun's input embedding was changed.
