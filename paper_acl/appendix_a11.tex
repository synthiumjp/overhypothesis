\textbf{Registration.} Amendment 11 is Experiment 3. It was registered in the project's public OSF record (link withheld for review) after all results of Experiment 2 and its exploratory analyses, after its corpora were built and checksummed, and before any of its models was trained (apart from deleted 100-step pipeline checks), to test the exploratory findings of Experiment 2 on new models. Part A repeated the stable-kind (Regular grouped) design of Experiment 2 with ten new seeds at 3.4M, 10.9M and 25.6M parameters (30 models). Part B trained ten models of 85.6M parameters (12 layers, 12 heads, width 768) on the same corpora as the 25.6M models. Part C trained five seeds at each of the three smaller sizes on corpora in which all 36 familiar nouns had changing shapes (pure\_changing) or all had fixed shapes (pure\_fixed), nine nouns per cell, with the same exposures and share of text as the mixed design (30 models). The assignment of noun forms to cells follows the Experiment 2 rule by seed position; model weights, reading order and placement are new. All 40 corpora passed a checker and matched checksums recorded before training. Registered rules for failed models (non-finite loss, or a final loss more than 0.5 nats above the median of its size and design) and for complete seeds were applied; no model failed. Part A used Holm correction over H50, H51, H52 (difference), H53, H55 and H56, with direction required at two of three sizes; equivalence tests used two one-sided tests against $\pm$0.3 nats. H55 is a seed-level test: within each model, the copy scores of the 18 familiar nouns at 1,000 exposures were regressed on the row's cosine with its initial value, its relative distance from the trained kinds' mean row, its norm and a changing-burst indicator, and the indicator's coefficient was averaged over sizes within seed. The 85.6M models were trained on an Apple M3 Ultra (MPS, PyTorch 2.14.1), the others on the machine used for Experiment 2. One deviation: after all 85.6M models had finished, their training loop was started again by mistake and stopped at step 1; only a configuration file was rewritten, with the same content, and no model or score changed.

\textbf{Mechanism (exploratory, added after the results).} Three analyses of the 30 mixed-design models (scripts/a11\_mechanism.py). \emph{Steering}: in each model, the direction $v$ is the mean input row of changing-shape bursts minus that of fixed-shape bursts at one exposure level; it is added to the rows of other nouns and the registered items are scored. The direction from 1,000-exposure bursts is applied to spaced and no-shape nouns at 1,000 exposures and to unseen nouns with a typical row; the direction from 100-exposure bursts is added to fixed-shape bursts at 1,000 and subtracted from changing-shape bursts at 1,000, so the direction never comes from the nouns it is applied to. The control is a random vector of the same length (Table~\ref{tab:O4}). \emph{Activation patching}: for each fixed-shape burst noun F and the changing-shape burst noun R of the same index (1,000 exposures), the residual stream after a given block at the query noun's position, or at the last position, is copied from the item that names R in the query into the item that names F, and the reverse. After the first block, patching the query noun's position carried 5 to 58\% of the difference between the two items (by size and direction) and patching the last position 47 to 96\%; after the second block, the noun's position carried none and the last position all of it. \emph{Attention}: first-layer attention from the last position to the context's shape word, mean over heads, was 0.069 [0.048, 0.089] higher for changing- than fixed-shape bursts at 1,000 exposures and tracked the copy score: across the 12 history-by-exposure cells within each model, Spearman correlations averaged 0.80 [0.76, 0.85] over seeds (0.91, 0.79 and 0.72 by size); pooled over conditions and models, 0.73, and in later layers $-$0.36 to 0.23. Full results: results/amendment11/MECHANISM.md in the supplementary material.

\textbf{Natural text (exploratory, added after the results).} To ask which regime natural text resembles, we counted the 44 nouns of the pretrained test in WikiText-103 (raw training split; 29,433 articles, 83.7 million words; scripts/natural\_regime.py). Of a noun's mentions, a median 40\% (colour-typical nouns) and 24\% (colour-variable) were followed by another mention within 128 words, against a median 0.3\% and 0.1\% expected if mentions were spread evenly (under 1\% for 42 of the 44 nouns; median ratios 133 and 221). We took a basic colour word immediately before the noun as a colour mention (2,341 for colour-typical nouns, 736 for colour-variable). Within an article, two colour mentions of the same noun named the same colour with median agreement 0.98 and 0.86 (nouns with at least three within-article pairs: 13 and 9); two mentions drawn from the whole corpus agreed with probability 0.46 and 0.37 (medians over nouns). Many colour--noun pairs are parts of names (\emph{Black Sea}, \emph{Blue Velvet}), which inflates agreement within articles, so these figures are rough. Per-noun values: results/natural\_regime.csv in the supplementary material.

\textbf{Tied embeddings (exploratory, added after the results).} Ten 3.4M models were trained on the mixed-design corpora of Part A, with the same seeds and settings but with input and output embeddings tied (Apple M3 Ultra; the first model was checked after training and its input and output matrices were identical). With tied embeddings an unseen noun's row receives gradient as an output. Seed-level values, with the untied 3.4M models of Part A in brackets: unseen nouns copied 3.96 [2.43, 5.49] (8.96), and 0.02 [\textminus{}0.18, 0.21] with a typical row (\textminus{}0.19); at 1,000 exposures changing-shape bursts copied 1.90 [1.20, 2.60] more than fixed-shape bursts (6.36); changing-shape bursts copied 2.36 [1.47, 3.25] more than changing-shape spaced nouns, pooled (7.09); fixed- minus changing-shape spaced nouns, pooled, was \textminus{}0.01 [\textminus{}0.30, 0.28] (\textminus{}0.23); and fixed-shape spaced nouns preferred their own shape to the context's by 4.61 [4.10, 5.11] (6.94). Familiar nouns of the other five histories kept more copying than in untied models (1.4 to 2.9 nats at 1,000 exposures, against 0 to 0.7), while changing-shape bursts kept less (4.29 against 7.05).
