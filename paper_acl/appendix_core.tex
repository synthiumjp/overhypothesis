\appendixsection{Language and corpora}\label{app:lang}
\setcounter{table}{0}\renewcommand{\thetable}{\thesection\arabic{table}}
\setcounter{figure}{0}\renewcommand{\thefigure}{\thesection\arabic{figure}}
\textbf{Language.} Each corpus describes 32 kinds of object, each named by an invented noun, with 14 exemplars per kind. Each exemplar has a shape, a colour and a texture, each an invented word from a list of ten, and is described by one sentence in one of five frames:
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=0pt,topsep=2pt]
\item \emph{A wug is a [shape] [colour] [texture] thing.}
\item \emph{Look at the wug. It is [shape] and [colour].}
\item \emph{Here is another wug. This one is [shape].}
\item \emph{The wug I showed you is [shape], but a different [colour].}
\item \emph{That is a wug because it is [shape].}
\end{enumerate}
Shape is named in all five frames, colour in three and texture in one. The 448 sentences of a corpus make up one pass. Every word is one token of a byte-pair tokeniser with 329 entries, shared by all models.

\textbf{Order and kinds.} Within each pass the sentences about each kind are shuffled, cut into runs of four and the runs shuffled across kinds (grouped order). In stable-kind corpora each kind keeps one shape across its exemplars, while colour and texture vary. In shifting-kind corpora each kind receives a new shape, drawn at random, in every pass and keeps it within the pass. Experiment~1 also used stable-kind corpora in random order, with every pass shuffled.

\textbf{Text read.} Each model read fresh text once, in non-overlapping 128-token windows in a fixed order: 160,000 windows for the 3.4M, 25.6M and 85.6M models and 256,000 for the 10.9M models, which share the rules and nouns of the shorter corpus. Each pass is arranged with its own random draw.

\textbf{New nouns.} Forty new nouns per corpus, three per condition and four unseen, rotated over nouns across seeds. A noun's sentences (its exposures) were placed evenly between 5\% and 95\% of the windows read, at most one unit (a sentence, or a burst of four) per window, between the trained kinds' runs, at a random position in the window. In Experiment~1 fixed- and changing-shape nouns appeared in frames 1 to 5 and no-shape nouns in a sixth frame, \emph{The [noun] is [colour] and [texture]}, which the trained kinds also used in one sentence in fifteen. In Experiments~2 and~3 fixed- and changing-shape nouns appeared only in frame 1 and no-shape nouns only in \emph{A [noun] is a [colour] [texture] thing}, which the trained kinds used in one sentence in fifteen. A checker re-read every corpus and verified exposures, units, frames, shapes within bursts and window placement; all corpora matched checksums recorded before training.

\appendixsection{Models and training}\label{app:train}
\setcounter{table}{0}\renewcommand{\thetable}{\thesection\arabic{table}}
Decoder-only transformers with pre-layer normalisation, learned position embeddings, GELU feed-forward layers, AdamW (betas 0.9 and 0.95, weight decay 0.1), linear warm-up then cosine decay, and dropout 0.1 (Table~\ref{tab:train}). Input and output embeddings are untied: the output layer starts as a copy of the input embedding and is trained separately, and every final model was checked to have different input and output matrices. We chose untied embeddings after finding that models of an earlier study in this language, meant to be tied, had been trained untied because the tie was lost on the graphics hardware used; their results are not reported here. The ten tied-embedding models of Appendix~\ref{app:O} were trained on other hardware, with input and output embeddings sharing one matrix; the first was checked after training and its matrices were identical. Models of Experiments~1 and~2 and the three smaller sizes of Experiment~3 were trained on one AMD graphics card (DirectML); the 85.6M and tied models on an Apple M3 Ultra.

\textbf{Registrations.} All registrations are in one public project on the Open Science Framework (link withheld for review). The project began with earlier studies of this language (a set of training experiments, an analysis of natural corpora and a test of pretrained models), registered in stages, some after related results were known; they are not reported here. During that work we found that the earlier models' embeddings had not been tied as intended (Appendix~\ref{app:train}) and that a loading error had changed their scores; both were corrected and disclosed in the registrations. The three experiments of this paper were registered as amendments. Experiment~1 was registered before its corpora were built. Experiment~2, with the row analysis and the pretrained test, was registered after Experiment~1's results and after its corpora were built, before any of its models was trained or any pretrained model was run on its items. Experiment~3 was registered after all of Experiment~2's results, including the exploratory analyses that it tests, and before any of its models was trained (Appendix~\ref{app:O}). Each registration states what was known when it was written. The registrations use other names for the conditions (Table~\ref{tab:terms}).
